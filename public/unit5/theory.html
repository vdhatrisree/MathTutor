1. Coordinate System Recap

A Cartesian coordinate system is formed by two perpendicular number lines: the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis. Their intersection point is called the origin O(0,0).

Every point in the plane is represented as an ordered pair (x, y), where x is the horizontal distance (abscissa) and y is the vertical distance (ordinate) from the origin.

Quadrants

The axes divide the plane into four quadrants:
Quadrant I: (+, +)  |  Quadrant II: (−, +)  |  Quadrant III: (−, −)  |  Quadrant IV: (+, −)

2. Distance Formula

The distance between two points A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) in a plane is given by the Distance Formula, derived from the Pythagorean theorem.

Distance Formula
d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
Note: The distance is always a non-negative value. When both points lie on the x-axis (y=0), the formula reduces to |x₂ − x₁|.
Example
Distance between A(1, 2) and B(4, 6):
d = √[(4−1)² + (6−2)²] = √[9 + 16] = √25 = 5 units
Distance from Origin: The distance of point P(x, y) from origin O(0,0) is √(x² + y²).

3. Section Formula (Internal Division)

If a point P divides the line segment joining A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in the ratio m : n, then the coordinates of P are:

Section Formula – Internal Division
P = ( (mx₂ + nx₁) / (m + n) , (my₂ + ny₁) / (m + n) )
m : n is the ratio in which P divides AB from A to B. The point P lies between A and B for internal division.
Example
Point dividing (1, 3) and (7, 9) in ratio 1:2:
x = (1×7 + 2×1)/(1+2) = (7+2)/3 = 3  |  y = (1×9 + 2×3)/3 = (9+6)/3 = 5
P = (3, 5)

4. Midpoint Formula

The midpoint is the point that divides a segment in the ratio 1:1. Substituting m = n = 1 in the section formula gives:

Midpoint Formula
M = ( (x₁ + x₂) / 2 , (y₁ + y₂) / 2 )
The midpoint is equidistant from both endpoints.
Example
Midpoint of (2, 4) and (8, 10):
M = ((2+8)/2, (4+10)/2) = (5, 7)

5. Area of a Triangle

Given three vertices A(x₁,y₁), B(x₂,y₂), C(x₃,y₃), the area of triangle ABC is:

Area of Triangle
Area = ½ |x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|
The absolute value ensures area is always positive. If the area = 0, the three points are collinear (lie on the same line).
Example
Area of triangle with A(0,0), B(4,0), C(0,3):
= ½ |0(0−3) + 4(3−0) + 0(0−0)| = ½ |0 + 12 + 0| = 6 sq. units

Centroid of a Triangle

The centroid (point of intersection of medians) is:

Centroid
G = ( (x₁+x₂+x₃)/3 , (y₁+y₂+y₃)/3 )
The centroid divides each median in the ratio 2:1 from vertex to midpoint of opposite side.

6. Slope of a Line

The slope (or gradient) of a line measures its steepness. For a line passing through A(x₁,y₁) and B(x₂,y₂):

Slope Formula
m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁) = tan θ
θ is the angle the line makes with the positive x-axis (inclination). The slope is undefined for vertical lines (x₁ = x₂). For horizontal lines, slope = 0.
  • If θ is acute (0° < θ < 90°), slope is positive → line rises left to right.
  • If θ is obtuse (90° < θ < 180°), slope is negative → line falls left to right.
  • If θ = 0°, slope = 0 → horizontal line.
  • If θ = 90°, slope is undefined → vertical line.

7. Conditions for Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Parallel Lines

Two lines are parallel if and only if their slopes are equal: m₁ = m₂ (and they have different y-intercepts).

Perpendicular Lines

Two lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of their slopes is −1: m₁ × m₂ = −1, which means m₂ = −1/m₁.

A horizontal line (slope = 0) is perpendicular to a vertical line (undefined slope). The condition m₁ × m₂ = −1 does not apply in this special case.

8. Equation of a Line

Slope-Intercept Form

Slope-Intercept Form
y = mx + c
m = slope of the line, c = y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis).

Point-Slope Form

Point-Slope Form
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
Used when we know the slope m and a point (x₁, y₁) on the line.

Two-Point Form

Two-Point Form
(y − y₁)/(y₂ − y₁) = (x − x₁)/(x₂ − x₁)
Used when two points on the line are known. Simplifies to point-slope form after finding the slope.

General Form

Any straight line can be written in the general form: ax + by + c = 0, where a, b, c are real constants and a, b are not both zero. The slope is m = −a/b and y-intercept is −c/b.

9. Collinearity of Three Points

Three points A, B, C are collinear (lie on the same straight line) if and only if the area of the triangle formed by them is zero.

Condition for Collinearity
x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂) = 0
This is equivalent to saying the slope of AB equals the slope of BC (or AC).
Example
Check if A(1,1), B(2,2), C(3,3) are collinear:
Area = ½|1(2−3) + 2(3−1) + 3(1−2)| = ½|−1 + 4 − 3| = ½|0| = 0 → Collinear ✓
