1. Euclid's Division Lemma

Euclid's Division Lemma

For any two positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that:
a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b
Here, a is the dividend, b is the divisor, q is the quotient, and r is the remainder.

This lemma is the foundation of the Euclidean algorithm used to find the HCF of two numbers.

Key Point: The remainder r always satisfies 0 ≤ r < b (the divisor). If r = 0, then b divides a exactly.
Example
Divide 17 by 5: 17 = 5 × 3 + 2
Here a = 17, b = 5, q = 3, r = 2. Since 0 ≤ 2 < 5, the lemma is satisfied.

2. HCF Using Euclid's Algorithm

Euclid's Algorithm repeatedly applies the Division Lemma to find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two numbers.

Steps:

  1. Apply the Division Lemma: a = bq + r.
  2. If r = 0, then HCF = b.
  3. If r ≠ 0, replace a with b and b with r, then repeat.
Step-by-step: HCF(252, 105)
Step 1: 252 = 105 × 2 + 42
Step 2: 105 = 42 × 2 + 21
Step 3: 42 = 21 × 2 + 0
Since remainder = 0, HCF(252, 105) = 21
The HCF is always the last non-zero remainder obtained in the Euclidean algorithm.

3. LCM and HCF Relationship

LCM × HCF = Product of Two Numbers

For any two positive integers a and b:
LCM(a, b) × HCF(a, b) = a × b

This property allows us to find the LCM if the HCF and the product of two numbers are known, or vice versa.

Example
HCF(12, 18) = 6, Product = 12 × 18 = 216
LCM = 216 ÷ 6 = 36
Note: This relationship holds only for two numbers. It does not directly extend to three or more numbers.

4. Arithmetic Progression (AP)

Arithmetic Progression

A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by adding a fixed number (called the common difference) to the previous term.

TermMeaning
First term (a)The first element of the sequence
Common difference (d)d = a₂ − a₁ = a₃ − a₂ (constant)
nth term (aₙ)aₙ = a + (n − 1)d
Sum of n terms (Sₙ)Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d]
Example
AP: 2, 5, 8, 11, … → a = 2, d = 3
10th term: a₁₀ = 2 + 9 × 3 = 29
Sum of 10 terms: S₁₀ = 10/2 × [4 + 27] = 5 × 31 = 155
If d > 0, the AP is increasing. If d < 0, it is decreasing. If d = 0, all terms are equal.

5. Sum Formulas for Natural Numbers

These are standard results for sums of powers of the first n natural numbers:

Sum of first n natural numbers

Σn = n(n + 1) / 2

Sum of squares of first n natural numbers

Σn² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6

Sum of cubes of first n natural numbers

Σn³ = [n(n + 1) / 2]²

Example — Sum of first 10 natural numbers
Σ(1 to 10) n = 10 × 11 / 2 = 55
Σ(1 to 10) n² = 10 × 11 × 21 / 6 = 385
Σ(1 to 10) n³ = [10 × 11 / 2]² = 55² = 3025
Notice that Σn³ = (Σn)². This is a beautiful identity called Nicomachus's theorem.

6. Geometric Progression (GP)

Geometric Progression

A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a fixed non-zero number called the common ratio.

TermMeaning
First term (a)The first element of the GP
Common ratio (r)r = a₂/a₁ = a₃/a₂ (constant, r ≠ 0)
nth term (aₙ)aₙ = a · rⁿ⁻¹
Sum of n terms (Sₙ), r ≠ 1Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1) / (r − 1)
Sum of n terms, r = 1Sₙ = na
Example
GP: 2, 6, 18, 54, … → a = 2, r = 3
5th term: a₅ = 2 × 3⁴ = 2 × 81 = 162
Sum of 4 terms: S₄ = 2(3⁴ − 1)/(3 − 1) = 2 × 80/2 = 80
The common ratio r can be found as r = any term ÷ its preceding term.

7. Special Sequences

Triangular Numbers

Numbers that can form a triangular dot pattern: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, …
The nth triangular number = n(n+1)/2

Square Numbers

Perfect squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, …
The nth square number = n²

Fibonacci Sequence

A sequence where each term is the sum of the two preceding terms: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, …

In a GP, the ratio of any term to the previous term is always equal to the common ratio r. In an AP, the difference of any term from the previous term is always equal to d.
