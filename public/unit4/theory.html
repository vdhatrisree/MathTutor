1. Similar Triangles

Similar Triangles

Two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. If △ABC ~ △DEF, then ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, ∠C = ∠F and AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD.

Criteria for Similarity

CriterionCondition
AA (Angle-Angle)If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.
SAS (Side-Angle-Side)If one angle is equal and the sides including that angle are proportional, the triangles are similar.
SSS (Side-Side-Side)If all three pairs of corresponding sides are proportional, the triangles are similar.
Key property: If △ABC ~ △DEF with ratio k, then: Area(△ABC)/Area(△DEF) = k² (ratio of areas = square of the ratio of corresponding sides).

2. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales' Theorem)

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle intersecting the other two sides at distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

In △ABC, if DE ∥ BC and DE intersects AB at D and AC at E, then:
AD/DB = AE/EC

Converse of BPT

If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

If AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE ∥ BC.

Example
In △ABC, DE ∥ BC. If AD = 3, DB = 5, AE = 4, find EC.
AD/DB = AE/EC → 3/5 = 4/EC → EC = 20/3
BPT is also called the Midpoint Theorem when the line passes through the midpoints (AD = DB), in which case DE = BC/2 and DE ∥ BC.

3. Pythagoras Theorem

Pythagoras Theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

If ∠C = 90°, then AB² = BC² + AC², or equivalently c² = a² + b².

Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

If in a triangle, the square of one side equals the sum of squares of the other two sides, then the angle opposite the longest side is a right angle.

If c² = a² + b², then the triangle is right-angled with ∠C = 90°.

Pythagorean Triples (common ones)
(3, 4, 5): 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²
(5, 12, 13): 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13²
(8, 15, 17): 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17²

5. Tangent to a Circle

Tangent–Radius Perpendicularity

The tangent to a circle at any point is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.
If PT is a tangent to a circle with centre O at point T, then OT ⊥ PT.

Tangents from an External Point

The lengths of the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
If PA and PB are tangents from external point P to a circle with centre O, then PA = PB.

Length of Tangent from External Point
If d = distance from external point P to centre O, and r = radius:
Length of tangent t = √(d² − r²)
This follows from the right triangle OTP where OT = r, OP = d, PT = t.
Since OT ⊥ PT, the triangle OTP is right-angled at T, giving us OP² = OT² + PT², hence PT = √(OP² − OT²).

6. Angle in a Semicircle

Angle in a Semicircle (Thales' Theorem)

The angle inscribed in a semicircle (the angle subtended by the diameter at any point on the circle) is always 90°.

If AB is a diameter and C is any point on the circle (not A or B), then ∠ACB = 90°.

Angle Subtended by a Chord

The angle subtended by a chord at the centre is twice the angle subtended by it at any point on the major arc.

∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB (where O is the centre)

Remember: Equal chords in the same circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

7. Alternate Segment Theorem

Alternate Segment Theorem (Tangent-Chord Angle)

The angle between a tangent to a circle and a chord drawn from the point of tangency equals the inscribed angle subtending the same arc on the opposite side.

If PT is a tangent at T and TC is a chord, then the angle between PT and TC equals the inscribed angle in the alternate segment.

Summary of Circle Theorems
1. Tangent ⊥ Radius at point of contact
2. Tangents from external point are equal
3. Angle in semicircle = 90°
4. Central angle = 2 × inscribed angle (same arc)
5. Angles in the same segment are equal
