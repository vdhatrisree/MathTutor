Chapter 4 — Key Formulas

All formulas needed for exams · Class 10 Samacheer Kalvi

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

BPT (Thales' Theorem)
AD/DB = AE/EC
In △ABC, if DE ∥ BC and D is on AB, E is on AC, then AD/DB = AE/EC.
Converse: If AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE ∥ BC.
BPT — Alternate Form
AD/AB = AE/AC = DE/BC
When DE ∥ BC, the entire side ratios are also equal. This helps find DE if the sides are known.

Similar Triangles

Ratio of Corresponding Sides
AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD = k (if △ABC ~ △DEF)
k is the scale factor. All three ratios of corresponding sides are equal.
Ratio of Areas of Similar Triangles
Area(△ABC) / Area(△DEF) = k² = (AB/DE)²
The ratio of areas of similar triangles equals the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
AA Similarity Criterion
∠A = ∠D AND ∠B = ∠E ⟹ △ABC ~ △DEF
If two angles of one triangle equal two angles of another, the triangles are similar (the third angle is automatically equal).

Pythagoras Theorem

Pythagoras Theorem
a² + b² = c²
c = hypotenuse (side opposite 90°), a and b are the other two sides.
Example: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²
Converse of Pythagoras
If c² = a² + b², then ∠C = 90°
If the sum of squares of two sides equals the square of the third side, the triangle is right-angled at the vertex opposite the longest side.

Tangents to a Circle

Tangent–Radius Perpendicularity
OT ⊥ PT (tangent at T, centre O)
The radius to the point of tangency is always perpendicular to the tangent. This creates a right angle at the point of contact.
Length of Tangent from External Point
t = √(d² − r²)
t = tangent length, d = distance from external point to centre, r = radius.
Derived from Pythagoras: d² = r² + t²
Equal Tangents from External Point
PA = PB (tangents from external point P)
The two tangent segments drawn from any external point to a circle are equal in length.
Key theorem Often tested in exams.

Circle Angle Theorems

Angle in a Semicircle
∠ACB = 90° (AB is diameter, C on circle)
Any angle inscribed in a semicircle (subtended by the diameter at the circumference) is a right angle.
Central Angle Theorem
∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB
The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, both subtended by the same arc.
A, B are on the circle, O is the centre, C is on the major arc.
Angles in the Same Segment
∠ACB = ∠ADB (C and D on same arc)
Angles inscribed in the same segment (on the same side of the chord) are equal.
